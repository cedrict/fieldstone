Our goal here is to introduce dilation in the governing equations.
Dilation implies a change in volume, which conflicts with the assumption of incompressibility. 
Nevertheless we find ourselves wanting to keep using the framework of incompressibility and 
so wish to account for the effects of dilation in the rhs of the mass and momentum conservation
equations. This is very similar to the dilation rate terms of \textcite{chpe15} (2015) -- see \stone 39.



\newpage
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section*{A) Manufactured solutions in 1d}

Let us denote by $\vec{\upnu}^0$ and $p^0$ the solution of the incompressible Stokes equations
given a buoyancy force and appropriate boundary conditions:
\begin{eqnarray}
-\vec\nabla p^0 +\vec\nabla\cdot[2\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0)] +\rho\vec{g} &=&\vec{0} \label{eq:dil1} \\
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu^0 &=& 0
\end{eqnarray}
Let us now consider the velocity field $\vec{\upnu}=\vec{\upnu}^0+\vec{\upnu}_d$
where $\vec{\upnu}_d$ is a user-defined velocity field function that is in $C^2$. 
We assume 2d Cartesian coordinates in $\Omega$, a domain of size $L_x \times L_y$. 






%____________________________
\subsection*{Generic case}

For now, let us take $\vec{\upnu}_d=(u_d(x,y) ,0)$.
The strain rate tensor is then given by 
\[
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu)= \dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0)
+ \dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)= 
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0) + 
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} & \frac12 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  \\
\frac12 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\]
Then 
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu 
= \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu)]
= \underbrace{\text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0)]}_{=  \vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu^0  =0}
+ \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)]
= \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x}
\]
This completes the search of the right hand side of the mass conservation equation.

We will also need the corresponding deviatoric strain rate tensor (here $D=3$, 
see discussions in Aspect on the topic
as \url{https://aspect-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/methods/basic-equations/2d-models.html}):
\[
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)
=\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d) - \frac{1}{D} \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)] {\bm 1}
=\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d) - \frac{1}{D} \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} {\bm 1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
(1-\frac1D) \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} & \frac12 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  \\
\frac12 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  & -\frac1D \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

%_____________________________________________________
\subsubsection*{Compensated approach}

We now wish to look at the momentum conservation equation with an additional rhs vector term 
$\vec{f}_d$ accounting for the dilation:
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2 \eta \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu)]  -\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{f}_d
\]
First, let us take $\eta=1$ to simplify the equation:
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu)]  -\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{f}_d
\]
or,
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  (\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0) +\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d) ) ]  
-\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{f}_d
\]
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:dil1} the equation above then simply becomes
\begin{equation}
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)  ]  = \vec{f}_d
\label{eq:dilation_rhs}
\end{equation}
or, 
\[
(f_x \quad f_y)
=
(\partial_x \quad \partial_y) \cdot \begin{pmatrix}
(2-\frac2D) \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} &  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  \\
 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y}  & -\frac2D \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
i.e., 
\begin{eqnarray}
f_x &=& \left(2-\frac2D\right) \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2}  +  \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial y^2} \nonumber\\
f_y &=& \left(1-\frac2D\right) \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x \partial y}  \label{f175:fxfy}
\end{eqnarray}
We here make the convenient assumption that $u_d$ is only a function of $x$, so that now
\begin{eqnarray}
f_x &=& (2-\frac2D) \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2}   \nonumber\\
f_y &=& 0
\end{eqnarray}
Taking $D=3$:
\begin{eqnarray}
f_x &=& \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2}   \nonumber\\
f_y &=& 0
\end{eqnarray}

We have to read this all as follows: There is a dilation term $\frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x}$ 
(which is a function of the $x$-coordinate only) which in turn generates a horizontal flow $(u_d,0)$
atop the Stokes solution $\vec\upnu^0$ and $p^0$ remains the pressure solution.

%-------------------------------------------
\paragraph{A remark about the choice of dilation term}

When designing a manufactured solution one could think of choosing the dilation rate 
function $du_d/dx$ to be constant in a band or an area and 
zero elsewhere. However, this poses a problem: the rhs of the momentum equation 
contains the term $d^2u_d/dx$, i.e. the gradient of the dilation rate. That quantity is 
ill-defined on the edges of such a band. 

One could then prescribe the dilation rate on the nodes and use the basis functions derivatives
to compute (an approximation of) the gradient of the dilation rate. 
When designing a manufactured solution it remains simpler to consider a smoother dilation 
band that circumvents that problem altogether. 


%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Uncompensated approach}
We then start again from 
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2 \eta \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu)]  -\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
with $\vec\upnu=\vec\upnu_d+\vec\upnu^0$. Note that the rhs remains zero in this case.
First, let us take $\eta=1$ again to simplify the equation:
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu)]  -\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
or,
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  (\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0) +\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d) ) ]  
-\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0)  ]  
+\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)  ]  
-\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
The second term of this equation is actually a vector, which we can compute as:
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)  ]  = 
\left(\begin{array}{c}
 \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2}   \\
 0
\end{array}\right)
\]
and which can also be written as the gradient of a quantity $p'$, i.e.
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)  ]  = 
\left(\begin{array}{c}
 \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2}   \\
 0
\end{array}\right)
=
\vec\nabla p' 
\qquad
\qquad
\text{with}
\qquad
 p'= \frac43 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x}   + const
\]
We can then write
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0)  ]  
+\vec\nabla p' -\vec\nabla p^0 + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
and we can define $\tilde{p}=p^0-p'$ so that we have
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu^0)  ]  
-\vec\nabla \tilde{p} + \rho \vec{g} = \vec{0}
\]
In the absence of rhs terms in the momentum equation
the calculated pressure accomodates the dilation.

\section*{B) Manufactured solutions in 2d}

%------------------------
\subsection*{Generic case}

Let us now assume/postulate $\vec{\upnu}_d=(u_d(x,y) , v_d(x,y))$.
The strain rate tensor is then given by 
\[
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu)
= \dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0)
+ \dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)
= 
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0) + 
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} 
& \frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  \\
\frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  
& \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}
\]
Then 
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu 
= \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu)]
= \underbrace{\text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu^0)]}_{=0}
+ \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)]
= \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y}
\]
and it follows that\footnote{I here set $D=3$.}
\[
\dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)
=\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d) - \frac{1}{3} \text{Tr}[\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d)] {\bm 1}
=\dot{\bm \varepsilon}(\vec\upnu_d) - \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y} \right)  {\bm 1}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac23 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} - \frac13 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y} 
&  \frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  \\
\frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  
& -\frac13 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} + \frac23 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

%
\subsubsection*{Compensated approach}

We start again from Eq.~\eqref{eq:dilation_rhs}
\begin{eqnarray}
\vec\nabla \cdot [2  \dot{\bm \varepsilon}^d(\vec\upnu_d)  ]  &=& \vec{f}_d \nn\\
(\partial_x \quad \partial_y) \cdot 
2
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac23 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} - \frac13 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y} 
&  \frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  \\
\frac12 \left(  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} \right)  
& -\frac13 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} + \frac23 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}
&=&(f_x \quad f_y) \nn\\
(\partial_x \quad \partial_y) \cdot 
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac43 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} - \frac23 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y} 
&  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x}   \\
  \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial x} 
& -\frac23 \frac{\partial u_d}{\partial x} + \frac43 \frac{\partial v_d}{\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}
&=&(f_x \quad f_y)
\end{eqnarray}
We can rewrite the equation above by component:
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2} - \frac23 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x \partial y} 
+ \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x \partial y} &=& f_x 
\nn\\
\frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x\partial y} + \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x^2}
-\frac23 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x \partial y} + \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial y^2} &=& f_y \nn
 \end{eqnarray}
and finally:
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial y^2} 
+ \frac13 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x \partial y}  &=& f_x 
\nn\\
\frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x^2}
+ \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial y^2} 
+ \frac13\frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x\partial y} 
&=& f_y \nn
\end{eqnarray}
We can easily verify that these expressions are identical to 
Eq.~\eqref{f175:fxfy} when $v_d=0$.

This leads to the following functions in the stone:
\begin{lstlisting}
def fx(x,y,Lx,Ly):
    return (4/3*d2ud_dx2(x,y,Lx,Ly) +d2ud_dy2(x,y,Lx,Ly) +1/3*d2vd_dxdy(x,y,Lx,Ly))

def fy(x,y,Lx,Ly):
    return (d2vd_dx2(x,y,Lx,Ly) +4/3*d2vd_dy2(x,y,Lx,Ly) +1/3*d2ud_dxdy(x,y,Lx,Ly))
\end{lstlisting}

\section*{Experiment 1: Wide vertical cosine hill dilation zone}

We postulate a dilation zone and then compute the corresponding velocity: 
\[
\frac{d u_d}{d x} = \frac12 \left(1-\cos(\frac{2 \pi x}{L_x}) \right)
\]
where $L_x$ is the width of the 1d domain.
The dilation zone is strong in the middle of the domain and zero on the sides. 
The dilation velocity $u_d(x)$ is then given by integration of the equation above
\[
u_d(x) = \frac12x - \frac{L}{4\pi  }\sin \left(\frac{2 \pi x}{L} \right) +C
\]
where $C$ is the integration constant.
Given the planar symmetry $u_d$ should be zero along the line $x=L_x/2$, so 
\[
0 = \frac{L}{4}  + C \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad C = -L/4
\]
Then in the end:
\[
u_d(x) = \frac12 \left( x- \frac{L}{2} - \frac{L}{2\pi }\sin (\frac{2 \pi x}{L}) \right)
\]
Finally we will also need
\[
\frac{d^2 u_d}{d x^2} = \frac{\pi}{L}  \sin \left(\frac{2 \pi x}{L} \right)
\]
The analytical expressions for $u_d$ and $du_d/dx$ are shown here for $L_x=2$:

\section*{Experiment 2: Simple case of a narrow vertical cosine hill dilation zone}

We now start from a different dilation zone:
\[
\frac{d u_d}{d x} = \frac12 \left(1+\cos\frac{ \pi (x-L/2)}{\delta} \right)
\]
for $|x-Lx/2|<\delta$, zero otherwise.
We then integrate to find
\[
u_d(x) = \frac12x + \frac12\frac{\delta }{\pi  }\sin  \frac{ \pi (x-L/2)}{\delta} +C
\]
we find again $C=-L/4$
so that 
\[
u_d(x) = \frac12 \left[ x-L/2 + \frac{\delta }{\pi  }\sin  \frac{ \pi (x-L/2)}{\delta} \right]
\]
for $|x-Lx/2|<\delta$. Outside, we have
$u_d=constant$. We of course require the velocity to be continuous at 
$x=L/2\pm \delta$ with:
\[
u_d(x=L/2+\delta) = \frac{\delta }{2} 
\qquad
u_d(x=L/2-\delta) = -\frac{\delta }{2} 
\]
Finally we will need
\[
\frac{d^2 u_d}{d x^2} = -\frac{\pi}{2\delta}  \sin \frac{ \pi (x-L/2)}{\delta}
\]

As above we set $L_x=2$, $L_y=1$. Free slip boundary conditions are imposed on the 
top and bottom boundaries. $u=0$ is also presribed at location $(x=L_x/2,y=L_y)$
so as to remove the translational nullspace. $\rho$ is set to zero and $\eta=1$.

\section*{Experiment 3: Simple case of two orthogonal cosine hills dilation zone}

We now further assume/postulate $\vec{\upnu}_d=(u_d(x) , v_d(y))$
and start from the dilation zones given by:
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{d u_d}{d x} &=& \frac12 \left(1+\cos\frac{ \pi (x-L_x/2)}{\delta_x}\right) \qquad |x-L_x/2|<\delta_x \nn\\
\frac{d v_d}{d y} &=& \frac12 \left(1+\cos\frac{ \pi (y-L_y/2)}{\delta_y}\right) \qquad |y-L_y/2|<\delta_y \nn 
\end{eqnarray}
and zero otherwise.
We then integrate to find (with the integration constant being $-L/4$ as in the 1d case, 
stemmimg from the necessity of the velocity field to be continuous inside and 
outside of the zones)
\begin{eqnarray}
u_d(x) &=& \frac12 \left[ x-L_x/2 + \frac{\delta_x }{\pi  }\sin  \frac{ \pi (x-L_x/2)}{\delta_x} \right] \nn\\
v_d(x) &=& \frac12 \left[ y-L_y/2 + \frac{\delta_y }{\pi  }\sin  \frac{ \pi (y-L_y/2)}{\delta_y} \right] \nn
\end{eqnarray}

\begin{center}

\includegraphics[width=5.7cm]{python_codes/fieldstone_175/results/exp3/v.png}

Finally we will need

\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{d^2 u_d}{d x^2} &=& -\frac{\pi}{2\delta_x}  \sin \frac{ \pi (x-L_x/2)}{\delta_x} \nn\\
\frac{d^2 u_d}{d x dy} &=& 0 \nn\\ 
\frac{d^2 u_d}{dy^2} &=& 0 \nn\\ 
\frac{d^2 v_d}{d y^2} &=& -\frac{\pi}{2\delta_y}  \sin \frac{ \pi (y-L_y/2)}{\delta_y} \nn\\
\frac{d^2 v_d}{d x dy} &=& 0 \nn\\ 
\frac{d^2 v_d}{dx^2} &=& 0 \nn
\end{eqnarray}

Given the nature of the solution (normal stress is not zero on the boundary)
we cannot not prescribe the velocity on the boundary. In this case we 
then prescribe the analytical velocity $u_d,v_d$ on all sides of the domain.
And since this in turn leads to a pressure nullspace, it is removed by 
requiring that the pressure fulfills the condition $\int_\Omega p dV =0$.

In the end this experiment is not truly 2d: it is made of 2 1d experiments 
added to one another, one in the $x$ direction, one in the $y$ direction.
The dilation velocity is prescribed on all sides of the domain.

More concretely, the terms in the rhs of the momentum equation are:
\begin{eqnarray}
f_x 
&=&
\frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial y^2} 
+ \frac13 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x \partial y} 
=
\frac43 \frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x^2} 
= 
- \frac43 \frac{\pi}{2\delta_x}  \sin \frac{ \pi (x-L_x/2)}{\delta_x} \nn\\
f_y 
&=& 
\frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial x^2}
+ \frac43 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial y^2} 
+ \frac13\frac{\partial^2 u_d}{\partial x\partial y} 
=\frac43 \frac{\partial^2 v_d}{\partial y^2}

\section*{Magma chamber with constant dilation (not implemented)}

Let us assume this is a 2d problem and the magma chamber is centered on 
the origin of the axis coordinates:

Let us assume we prescribe there a dilation term so that in the chamber $\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu = {\cal D}$.
In polar coordinates we have
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r}
\] 
This leads to write 
\[
\frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r}
={\cal D} r 
\qquad
\Rightarrow
\qquad
r \upnu_r + C
=
\frac12 {\cal D} r^2 
\]
where $C$ is an integration constant.
We then obtain 
\[
\upnu_r(r) = \frac12 {\cal D} r +\frac{C}{r}
\]
Since the velocity must be finite in the middle of the chamber (i.e. for $r=0$)
then we must set $C=0$ so that in the end:
\[
\boxed{
\upnu_r(r) = \frac12 {\cal D} r 
}
\]
From this we can compute 
\[
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
= \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r} 
= \frac12 {\cal D} 
\]
\[
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
= \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ...
= \frac12 {\cal D} 
\] 
Sanity check: 
$
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
+
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
= \frac12 {\cal D} 
+ \frac12 {\cal D} 
= {\cal D}
$
We find in the literature that the $r$ component of the momentum equation in 
this coordinate system is given by
\[
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} + 
\frac{1}{r}
\frac{\partial \sigma_{r\theta}}{\partial \theta} 
%\frac{\partial \sigma_{rz}}{\partial z} 
+ \frac{1}{r} (\sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}) + \rho g_r= 0
\]
We assume that gravity is zero, and since the problem is axially symmetric the second 
term is zero ($\partial_\theta \rightarrow 0$).
We are then left with:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:meh}
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} + 
%\frac{\partial \sigma_{rz}}{\partial z} 
 \frac{1}{r} (\sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}) = 0
\end{equation}
We have 
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d &=&  \frac12 {\cal D} -\frac13 {\cal D}  = \frac16 {\cal D} \nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  &=&  \frac12 {\cal D} -\frac13 {\cal D}  = \frac16 {\cal D} \nn
\end{eqnarray}
so that 
\begin{eqnarray}
\sigma_{rr} &=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d = -p + 2 \eta \frac{1}{6} {\cal D} 
= -p + \eta \frac13 {\cal D} \nn\\
\sigma_{\theta\theta} &=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = -p + 2 \eta \frac{1}{6} {\cal D} 
= -p + \eta \frac13 {\cal D} \nn
\end{eqnarray}
Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:meh}  becomes
\[
-\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} =0
\]
which means that {\it the pressure is constant inside the chamber}.
However we would need more information about the domain/physics/boundary conditions
outside the chamber to be able to specify this constant.

\section*{Experiment 7: magma chamber with radial dilation}



Let us now assume that the dilation prescribed in the chamber is no more constant 
but given by 
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu = {\cal D} r
\]
This leads to write 
\[
\frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r} = {\cal D} r^2 
\qquad
\Rightarrow
\qquad
r \upnu_r +C =  \frac13 {\cal D} r^3 
\]
For the same reason as before we set $C=0$ so that 
\[
\boxed{
\upnu_r(r) = \frac13 {\cal D} r^2
}
\]
Since $\vec\upnu = \upnu_r \vec{e}_r$ and $\vec{e}_r=\cos\theta \vec{e}_x + \sin\theta \vec{e}_y$
the the velocity is as follows in Cartesian coordinates:
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& \upnu_r \cos\theta \\ 
\upnu_y &=& \upnu_r \sin\theta 
\end{eqnarray}
Since $\cos\theta = \frac{x}{r}$ and $\sin\theta = \frac{y}{r}$ then
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& \frac13 {\cal D} r^2 \frac{x}{r} = \frac13 {\cal D}  r x \\ 
\upnu_y &=& \frac13 {\cal D} r^2 \frac{y}{r} = \frac13 {\cal D}  r y 
\end{eqnarray}
From this we can compute
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r} 
= \frac23 {\cal D} r \\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ...
= \frac13 {\cal D} r
\end{eqnarray}
Again the sanity check yields 
$
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} +\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
= \frac23 {\cal D} r + \frac13 {\cal D} r = {\cal D} r
$
as expected.

The deviatoric strain rate tensor diagonal terms are then given by:
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d  &=& 
\dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  {\cal D} r 
=\frac13  {\cal D} r  \nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  &=&
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  -\frac13  {\cal D} r = 0 
\end{eqnarray}
we then need
\begin{eqnarray}
\sigma_{rr} &=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d  
= -p +  \frac23 \eta {\cal D}r \nn\\ 
\sigma_{\theta\theta} &=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} ^d 
= -p 
\end{eqnarray}
then we start again from Eq.~\eqref{eq:meh}, and obtain this time
\[
-\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
+  \frac23 \eta {\cal D} 
+ \frac23 \eta {\cal D} 
=0
\]
or,
\[
-\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
+  \frac43 \eta {\cal D} 
=0
\]
and finally 
\[
p(r) =  \frac43 \eta {\cal D}  r + C 
\]
where $C$ is an integration constant. 
This time the pressure is linear in $r$ inside the chamber.  



\paragraph{outside the chamber}

Outside the chamber there is no dilation, and the velocity divergence is zero, 
so that 
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r} = 0
\]
i.e. $r \upnu_r = C$, or $\upnu_r(r) = C/r$.

Assuming the chamber has a radius $R$, the velocity must be continuous at this 
location $r=R$, so we must have 
\[
\frac{1}{3} {\cal D} R^2 = \frac{C}{R} 
\qquad
\Rightarrow
\qquad
C = \frac{1}{3} {\cal D} R^3 
\]
so the velocity outside the chamber is then 
\[
\boxed{
\upnu_r(r) = \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r} 
}
\]
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& \upnu_r \cos\theta = \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r} \frac{x}{r} = \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r^2} x \\
\upnu_y &=& \upnu_r \sin\theta = \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r} \frac{y}{r} = \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r^2} y 
\end{eqnarray}



Let's go again computing the strain rate components:
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r} 
= - \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r^2} 
\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ...
= \frac{{\cal D} R^3 } {3r^2} 
\end{eqnarray}

Obviously $\dot\varepsilon_{rr}  + \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}=0$
so that $\dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d = \dot\varepsilon_{rr}$ 
and $\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d=\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}$. 

The stress components then write
\begin{eqnarray}
\sigma_{rr} 
&=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d  
= -p - \frac{2{\cal D} R^3 \eta } {3r^2}  \\
\sigma_{\theta\theta} 
&=& -p + 2 \eta \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  
= -p + \frac{2{\cal D} R^3 \eta } {3r^2}  
\end{eqnarray}
We have 
\[
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} 
= -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}  + 4 \frac{{\cal D} R^3 \eta } {3r^3}  
\]
\[
\frac{1}{r} (\sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}) 
= -  4 \frac{{\cal D} R^3 \eta } {3r^3}  
\]
In the end, combining these terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:meh}
we obtain:
\[
-\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = 0
\] 
so that the pressure is constant outside the chamber.

\section*{Experiment 9: magma chamber with ring around it}

This is experiment 9 of stone 175.
Let us now assume that the central disc has radius $R_1$ and is characterised
by a dilation ${\cal D} r$ as before.

The solution outside the disc is then $\upnu_r(r)= {\cal D} R_1^3/3r$.
We now add a ring bound by radii $R_2$ and $R_3$ with a different dilation rate term
yet to be determined. We introduce such a ring in order to recover a zero
velocity outside of it and therefore make the boundary conditions on the sides of
the domain very simple. 
Since the velocity field must be continuous we must then have
$\upnu_r(R_2)= {\cal D} R_1^3/3R_2$ and we impose
$\upnu_r(R_3)= 0$. 
We additionally require the velocity to be linear in the ring, so that we arrive at
\[
\upnu_r(r)=\frac{r-R_3}{R_2-R_3} \frac{{\cal D} R_1^3}{3R_2}
\]
Let us define $H=\frac{1}{R_2-R_3} \frac{{\cal D} R_1^3}{3R_2}$ so that 
\[
\upnu_r(r)=H(r-R_3)
\]
inside the ring. We then have
\[
\vec\nabla\cdot \vec\upnu = 
\frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r} 
= \frac{1}{r} H (2r-R_3) 
= \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H 
\]
Again the Cartesian components of the velocity are given by
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& \upnu_r \cos\theta \\ 
\upnu_y &=& \upnu_r \sin\theta 
\end{eqnarray}
and since
\[
\cos\theta = \frac{x}{r},
\qquad
\sin\theta = \frac{y}{r} 
\]
then 
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& H(r-R_3) \frac{x}{r}  \\
\upnu_y &=& H(r-R_3) \frac{y}{r}  
\end{eqnarray}





From this we can compute the strain rate in the ring:
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r}  =H \\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ...
= H \left(1 - \frac{R_3}{r} \right)
\end{eqnarray}
Again the sanity check yields 
\[
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} +\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
= H + H \left(1 - \frac{R_3}{r} \right)
= \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H 
\]
as expected.

The deviatoric strain rate tensor diagonal terms are then given by:

\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d  
&=& \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H  \nn\\
&=& H  -\frac13  \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H \nn \\
&=& \frac13\left[ 3  -  \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right) \right] H \nn \\
&=& \frac13\left( 1+ \frac{R_3}{r} \right) H  
\nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  
&=& \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  -\frac13   \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H  \nn\\
&=&  H \left(1 - \frac{R_3}{r} \right)-\frac13   \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  H  \nn\\
&=& \frac13  \left[  3\left(1 - \frac{R_3}{r} \right)-  \left(2-\frac{R_3}{r}\right)  \right] H \nn \\
&=& \frac13  \left(  1 - 2\frac{R_3}{r}  \right) H 
\end{eqnarray}
Then
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{1}{r}( \sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta} )
&=& 
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left( \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d   - \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  \right) \nn\\
&=& 
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left[
\frac13\left( 1+ \frac{R_3}{r} \right) H  
-
\frac13  \left(  1 - 2\frac{R_3}{r}  \right) H  
\right] \nn\\
&=& \frac{1}{r} \frac{2\eta}{3} \left[ 3\frac{R_3}{r}    \right] H  \nn\\
&=&  2\eta \frac{R_3}{r^2}   H  \nn\\
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} 
&=&
- \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
+ 2\eta \frac{\partial }{\partial r}  \frac13\left( 1+ \frac{R_3}{r} \right) H  \nn\\
&=&
- \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} - \frac{2 \eta}{3} \frac{R_3}{r^2}   H  
\end{eqnarray}


Then
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} +\frac{1}{r}( \sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta} )
&=&
- \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} - \frac{2 \eta}{3} \frac{R_3}{r^2}   H  
+ 2\eta \frac{R_3}{r^2}   H  \nn\\
&=& - \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} + \frac{4 \eta}{3} \frac{R_3}{r^2}   H  
\end{eqnarray}
so 
\[
p(r) \propto -\frac{4 \eta}{3} \frac{R_3}{r}   H  
\]

\section*{Experiment 11: New radially symmetric benchmark}





Let us postulate ${\cal D}(r)=(r-1)^2 d(r)$ on $r\in [0:1]$ (we thereby ensure that the dilation 
and its derivative is zero at $r=1$) and zero outside. Let us further take $d(r)=ar^3+br^2+cr+e$.
This yields:
\begin{eqnarray}
{\cal D}(r)
&=&(r-1)^2 (ar^3+br^2+cr+e) \nn\\
&=&(r^2-2r+1) (ar^3+br^2+cr+e) \nn\\
&=& ar^5 +(b-2a) r^4+(c-2b+a)r^3+(e-2c+b)r^2 +(c-2e)r + e
\end{eqnarray}


In order to be able to apply no-slip boundary conditions on all sides of the domain, 
we must insure that the overal effect of dilation (positive and negative) is zero 
in the disc, or
\begin{eqnarray}
0 &=& \iint {\cal D}(r) \; r dr d\theta \nn\\ 
&=& 2 \pi \int_0^1 {\cal D}(r) \; r dr \nn\\
&=& \int_0^1 {\cal D}(r) \; r dr  \nn\\
%&=& \int_0^1 (r-1)^2 d(r) \;  r dr  \nn\\
%&=& \int_0^1 (r-1)^2 (ar^3+br^2+cr+e) \;  r dr  \nn\\
%&=& \int_0^1 (r^2-2r+1) (ar^4+br^3+cr^2+er) \;  dr  \nn\\
%&=& \int_0^1 ( ar^6+br^5+cr^4+er^3 -2ar^5-2br^4-2cr^3-2er^2 + ar^4+br^3+cr^2+er ) \;  dr  \nn\\
&=& \int_0^1 [ ar^6 +(b-2a) r^5+(c-2b+a)r^4+(e-2c+b)r^3 +(c-2e)r^2 + er ] \;  dr  \nn\\
&=& \frac17 a +(b-2a)\frac16 +(c-2b+a) \frac15 +(e-2c+b) \frac14  +  (c-2e) \frac13  +\frac12 e   \nn\\
&=& 
a \left( \frac17 - \frac13 +\frac15 \right) +
b \left( \frac16 -\frac25 +\frac14  \right) +
c \left( \frac15 -\frac12 +\frac13  \right) +
e \left( \frac14 -\frac23 +\frac12 \right) \nn\\
&=& \frac{1}{105}a
+\frac{1}{60} b
+ \frac{1}{30} c
+\frac{1}{12}e \nn\\
&=& \frac{1}{420} (4a +7b +14c +35e) \label{eq:420} 
\end{eqnarray}





We can now proceed to compute the velocity field.
We must solve:
\[
\vec\nabla \cdot \vec\upnu = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r} =  {\cal D}(r)
\qquad
\Rightarrow
\qquad
\frac{\partial (r \upnu_r) }{\partial r} = r {\cal D}(r)
\]
i.e.
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_r (r) 
&=& \frac{1}{r} \int r {\cal D}(r) dr \nn\\
%&=& \frac{1}{r} \int r (r-1)^2 (ar^3+br^2+cr+e)  dr  \nn\\
&=& \frac{1}{r} \int [ ar^6 +(b-2a) r^5+(c-2b+a)r^4+(e-2c+b)r^3 +(c-2e)r^2 + er ] \;  dr  \nn\\
&=& \frac{1}{r} 
\left[ \frac{r^7}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{r^6}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{r^5}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{r^4}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{r^3}{3}  
+\frac{r^2}{2} e   + const \right]  \nn\\
&=& 
\frac{r^6}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{r^5}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{r^4}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{r^3}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{r^2}{3}  
+\frac{r}{2} e 
+ \frac{const}{r}
\end{eqnarray}
Since the velocity must be finite at $r=0$ we must enforce $const=0$.
In the end 

\[
\boxed{
\upnu_r (r) 
=
\frac{r^6}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{r^5}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{r^4}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{r^3}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{r^2}{3}  
+\frac{r}{2} e 
}
\]
Remember that we also have 
\begin{eqnarray}
\upnu_x &=& \upnu_r \cos\theta = \upnu_r \frac{x}{r} \\ 
\upnu_y &=& \upnu_r \sin\theta = \upnu_r \frac{y}{r} 
\end{eqnarray}


From this we can compute
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r} \nn\\
&=&  
\frac{6r^5}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{5r^4}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{4r^3}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{3r^2}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{2r}{3}  
+\frac{1}{2} e 
\nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ... \nn\\
&=& 
\frac{r^5}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{r^4}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{r^3}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{r^2}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{r}{3}  
+\frac{1}{2} e 
\end{eqnarray}
Again the sanity check yields 

\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} +\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
&=&
\frac{6r^5}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{5r^4}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{4r^3}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{3r^2}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{2r}{3}  
+\frac{1}{2} e  \nn\\
&+& \frac{r^5}{7} a 
+(b-2a)\frac{r^4}{6} 
+(c-2b+a) \frac{r^3}{5} 
+(e-2c+b) \frac{r^2}{4}
+(c-2e) \frac{r}{3}  
+\frac{1}{2} e \nn\\
&=&  
a r^5  
+(b-2a) r^4
+(c-2b+a) r^3
+(e-2c+b) r^2 
+(c-2e) r 
+ e  \nn\\
&=& {\cal D}(r)
\end{eqnarray}
as expected.



The deviatoric strain rate tensor diagonal terms are then given by:

\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d  
&=& \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  {\cal D}(r)  
= \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13 ( \dot\varepsilon_{rr} + \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} ) 
= \frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
\nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  
&=& \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  -\frac13  {\cal D}(r)  
= \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  -\frac13 ( \dot\varepsilon_{rr} + \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} ) 
= -\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{rr} + \frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  
\end{eqnarray}

Then
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{1}{r}( \sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta} )
&=& 
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left( \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d   - \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}^d  \right) \nn\\
&=& 
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left(
\frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
+\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{rr} - \frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
\right)  
\nn\\
&=& 
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left( \dot\varepsilon_{rr}   - \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  \right) \nn\\
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} 
&=& - \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} + 2\eta \frac{\partial }{\partial r} \dot\varepsilon_{rr}^d \nn\\
&=& - \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
+ 2\eta \frac{\partial }{\partial r}
\left(  \frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} \right) 
%&=& - \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
%+\frac43 \eta \frac{\partial }{\partial r}  \dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
% -\frac23 \eta  \frac{\partial }{\partial r}  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
\end{eqnarray}

and the $r$-component of the momentum equation (in the absence of gravity forces) writes
\begin{eqnarray}
0&=& 
\frac{\partial \sigma_{rr}}{\partial r} +\frac{1}{r}( \sigma_{rr} - \sigma_{\theta\theta} )\nn\\
&=&
- \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} 
+ 2\eta 
\frac{\partial }{\partial r}
\left(  \frac23 \dot\varepsilon_{rr}  -\frac13  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} \right) 
+
\frac{1}{r} 2\eta \left( \dot\varepsilon_{rr}   - \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}  \right) 
\end{eqnarray}
or, 
\begin{eqnarray}
\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}  
&=& 2 \eta 
\left[
\frac23 \underbrace{\frac{\partial }{\partial r}   \dot\varepsilon_{rr} }_{M} 
-\frac13 \underbrace{ \frac{\partial }{\partial r}  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} }_{N}
+ \underbrace{ \frac{1}{r} \dot\varepsilon_{rr}   }_{P}
- \underbrace{ \frac{1}{r}\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} }_{Q} 
\right]
\end{eqnarray}

For simplicity we write the radial velocity as follows:
\[
\upnu_r(r) = A  r^6 + B r^5 + C r^4 + D r^3 + E  r^2 + Fr
\]
with 
\[
A=\frac{a}{7}
\qquad
B=\frac16(b-2a)
\qquad
C=\frac15(a-2b+c)
\qquad
D=\frac14(b-2c+e)
\qquad
E=\frac13(c-2e)
\qquad
F=\frac{1}{2}e
\]



From this we can compute
\begin{eqnarray}
\dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& \frac{\partial \upnu_r}{\partial r} 
=  6A  r^5 + 5B r^4 + 4C r^3 + 3D r^2 + 2E  r + F
\nn\\
\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& \frac{\upnu_r}{r} + ... 
= A  r^5 + B r^4 + C r^3 + D r^2 + E  r + F
\nn\\
M = \frac{\partial }{\partial r}   \dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=& 30A  r^4 + 20B r^3 + 12C r^2 + 6D r + 2E    
\nn\\
N = \frac{\partial }{\partial r}  \dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} 
&=&  5A  r^4 + 4B r^3 + 3C r^2 + 2D r + E 
\nn\\
P=\frac{1}{r} \dot\varepsilon_{rr} 
&=&  6A  r^4 + 5B r^3 + 4C r^2 + 3D r + 2E  + F/r 
\nn\\
Q=\frac{1}{r}\dot\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}
&=& A  r^4 + B r^3 + C r^2 + D r + E  + F/r
\end{eqnarray}
and finally 


\begin{eqnarray}
\frac23 M - \frac13 N+P - Q 
%&=& \frac13 (2 M - N+3P - 3Q )\nn\\ 
%&=& \frac23 \left( 30A  r^4 + 20B r^3 + 12C r^2 + 6D r + 2E \right) \nn\\
%&-& \frac13 \left( 5A  r^4 + 4B r^3 + 3C r^2 + 2D r + E  \right) \nn\\

%&-&  \left( A  r^4 + B r^3 + C r^2 + D r + E  + F/r \right) \nn\\
&=& \frac{70}{3} A r^4 + 16 B r^3  + 10C r^2 + \frac{16}{3} Dr + 2E  
\end{eqnarray}
And in the end:
\[
p(r) = 2\eta \left(  \frac{14}{3} A r^5 + 4B r^4  + \frac{10}{3} C r^3 + \frac{8}{3}Dr^2 + 2Er + Const \right)
\]

We enforce $p(r=1)=0$, i.e.
\[
p(r=1) = 2\eta \left(  \frac{14}{3} A + 4B  + \frac{10}{3} C  + \frac{8}{3}D + 2E + Const \right) = 0
\]
\[
\frac{14}{3} A + 4B  + \frac{10}{3} C  + \frac{8}{3}D + 2E + Const = 0

+ \frac{10}{3} \frac15(a-2b+c)
